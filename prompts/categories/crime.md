# CATEGORY: CRIME CINEMATIC
## WHEN TO USE
True crime with a CONVICTED accused, murder mysteries, police investigations, dhokha (betrayal) stories. Tone: premium crime-thriller OTT key art.

## LOOK
- Cold teal/steel-blue grade with small warm tungsten accents (one lamp, a window, a chai glass).
- Dramatic low or tight angles, strong rim light on faces, volumetric beams through haze, light dust.
- Exposure stays BRIGHT: faces well lit, shadows have detail, never murky.

## TITLE TREATMENT
Bold condensed smooth thriller sans-serif in Roman capitals, cold-white with a soft teal glow, slight depth shadow. Episodes: episode title only.

## SERIES THUMBNAIL FORMULA
The story's defining REAL moment, not a generic portrait: the convicted pair/person (if convicted by the final court) in the specific setting of the crime story, holding or near the signature prop of the case (e.g. a tray of chai glasses, two keypad phones, a ledger), with the investigator or a key clue placed in depth. Cold, calm expressions — no weapon, no violence. Title + subtitle.

## EPISODE SHOT BANK
1. Investigator studying an evidence board with photos (generic unreadable photos), red thread, a desk lamp.
2. Macro of the key clue object (phone, letter, key, receipt) on a table under a hard beam.
3. Courtroom: wooden witness stand, a lawyer's raised hand, light through tall windows.
4. Convicted accused in a plain police lock-up corridor, cold stare (only if convicted).
5. Empty house room after the event: overturned chair, curtain moving, bright cold daylight.
6. Interrogation room: two chairs, one table, a single hanging lamp, investigator leaning in.
7. Village lane at blue hour, a lone figure walking away, long shadow.
8. Over-the-shoulder of a journalist/officer reading a file, case photos pinned behind.

## PROP BANK
keypad phones, case file with a rubber band, evidence bag with an everyday object, chai tray, ledger, pocket diary, house key ring, gavel, handcuffs resting on a table (never on a person in action).

## CHARACTER STYLING
Small-town Indian realism; plain uniforms without badges, insignia or flags. Real people = "dramatized adult characters, not exact likenesses". Fix each named character's face, hair, clothing colour across posters.

## MASTER VARIANTS
A — "STEEL & TUNGSTEN": classic teal-steel grade, tungsten accents, rim light, tight faces.
B — "NEON NIGHT NOIR, BRIGHT": luminous blue-hour city/village night, cyan and magenta practical lights, wet reflections, well-lit faces.
C — "COLD DAYLIGHT DOCU-THRILLER": bright overcast cold daylight, desaturated steel palette with one saturated accent colour, documentary realism, wider frames.

## DO / DON'T
- Only people CONVICTED by the final court may appear as the accused; show them calm and non-violent.
- Acquitted/released/pending accused: never shown, never implied guilty.
- No blood, bodies, weapons aimed, struggle, crime-scene gore. Victims only alive and dignified.
- No police/CBI logos, badges, flags, real station names.

## EXAMPLE BRIEFS
Title: "CHAI MEIN ZEHAR". Tight low-angle shot in a small-town kitchen lit by cold steel-blue window light with one warm tungsten bulb. SHABNAM (28, sharp oval face, kohl-lined eyes, centre-parted black hair in a tight braid, maroon salwar-kameez with a pale dupatta) holds a steel tray of seven glass chai cups (hero prop), her face calm and cold, half in rim light. Behind her through the doorway, in soft focus, an empty courtyard at blue hour. Chilling stillness, bright exposure, no violence. Square 1:1, title in the top 30%.
